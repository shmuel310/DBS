the timeline

The seventeen weeks of this course are spread across about three and a half years of Jesus' ministry, and this page shows roughly where each one falls. John's Gospel anchors the story with four Passovers and a couple of named feasts, and here and there a writer tells us "the next day" or "after six days", so those points are reasonably firm. In between them the order is clear but the spacing is an estimate. Read it as a guide to how those years may have unfolded rather than a record of dates: enough to show the progression, and measured from the baptism rather than any calendar.

how the order and the timing are worked out

The order of events follows Cox and Easley's Harmony of the Gospels, which is the same ordering the course itself uses. The full harmony, all 184 events laid out across Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, is online at harmony.darrenrowse.com.

The gospels never give a date, and scholars disagree about the calendar years by as much as three, so this timeline leaves calendar dates out altogether. Everything is measured from the baptism instead, which is the one thing all the accounts agree begins the story.

The length comes from John's Gospel. John names three Passovers: the temple clearing, the feeding of the five thousand, and the last one at the crucifixion. He also mentions a feast in chapter 5 without saying which one, and there are good reasons to think a fourth Passover falls in that stretch. That is what stretches the ministry to about three and a half years rather than two and a half. Those points, plus Tabernacles in chapter 7 and the winter festival in chapter 10, are the pegs everything else hangs between.

The timing follows Harold Hoehner's chronology in the appendix to the same harmony, which puts the baptism in the summer or autumn of one year and the crucifixion on a Friday three and a half years later. Worth knowing that the harmony's own editors prefer a crucifixion about three years earlier than Hoehner does. Two views in the one book is a fair picture of the state of the question, and another reason this page counts from the baptism rather than naming years.

Inside those pegs the order is firm, but the spacing is an estimate. When you see "about 2 years 9 months in", read it as "somewhere in this stretch of the story", not as a measured date. The gaps between the weeks and the order of events are the parts worth teaching from.
